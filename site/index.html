01 / 02 · From physical demand to a market outcome

A useful relationship.
A separate prediction test.

Weather and electricity usage are connected. The research asked whether a modest regression could capture that relationship—and whether genuinely archived forecasts also helped predict prices.

01Retrospective demand modeling

Temperature explained demand
better when the model could bend.

A piecewise-linear temperature spline improved on calendar effects. A simple straight-line temperature term did not.

39.2%lower mean absolute error
436.38 → 265.13 MW
Scope 43,700 matched hourly observations across five years; 8,654 evaluation hours in 2025. Uses observed weather, so this is demand estimation—not a demonstrated day-ahead demand forecast.
What went into this model?

NYISO hourly NYC/Zone J load and NOAA LaGuardia observed temperature. Calendar controls included hour of week, annual Fourier terms, hour-by-season interactions and observed holidays. The spline variant was selected using 2024 MAE.

Temperature added useful predictive information versus this calendar baseline. The result is an association, not a causal estimate or a claim of a novel physical mechanism.

02Archived-forecast price prediction

Available weather forecasts
helped estimate a future price.

A separate model predicted next-day evening day-ahead electricity prices using calendar features and forecast temperature.

16.6%lower mean absolute error
$35.16 → $29.33/MWh
Scope 1,809 timing-eligible dates across five years; 362 evaluation dates in 2025. Calendar-only baseline; no price-history or commercial-forecast comparison.
How did we check forecast timing?

At 04:00 New York time on D−1, estimate the D18:00–19:00 price. Use the HRRR 06 UTC operational forecast, with a 40/41-hour lead, at a grid point near LaGuardia. Included files had embedded issue/valid metadata and archive timestamps before a five-minute cutoff buffer.

Seventeen dates were excluded. Realized future temperatures were never substituted. Archive timestamps are availability evidence, not a complete historical first-publication ledger. “Finalized” refers to the retrieved corrected/latest price archives.

The important pivot

A better estimate of a price level does not automatically identify a profitable trade. Trading required a new target: the real-time price minus the day-ahead price.

Why isn’t a positive total enough?

The selected rule’s seven-day block-bootstrap interval for mean daily gross payoff is −$8.10 to +$18.29. The incremental interval versus calendar is −$0.72 to +$6.04/day. Both cross zero; 14/28-day sensitivity checks also cross zero.

Only three months were profitable. Linear temperature was selected on 2024 gross payoff; the unselected spline’s better 2025 result was not promoted after inspecting that year. These intervals describe the evaluated year, not model-refitting uncertainty or future-year reliability.

What was verified?

Older-cycle forecasts were available and verified for 1,823 of 1,826 dates. Three multipart timestamps did not prove completed availability and were excluded. Joining with the original current-forecast gate retained 362 evaluation dates.

Data sourcing & the feasibility stop

NYISO hourly load and zonal electricity prices; NOAA GHCNh observed LaGuardia temperature; operational HRRR forecast temperature. Data were aligned across UTC, local time and daylight-saving transitions, with coverage and exclusion records.

An initial price-history extension stopped because complete historical input versions could not be verified. The revised price question excluded lagged-price inputs, load and demand-model outputs. Corrected prices served as outcomes, not unavailable model inputs.

Models, benchmarks & uncertainty

NumPy ordinary least squares, calendar features, linear temperature and piecewise-linear splines. Splines and transforms use fitting data. The price experiment selects by validation MAE; the spread experiment selects by validation gross payoff. These are distinct objectives.

Demand uses 2,000 circular block-bootstrap replicates; price and trading use 5,000. Seven-day intervals are primary, with 14/28-day sensitivity checks. Simple baselines, same-date comparisons and outlier concentration remain visible.

Limitations & what this project does not establish

One NYC zone, one weather location and one final evaluation year limit generalization. Calendar-only predictive baselines omit price history, fuel costs, outages and wider system conditions. The study does not establish causality, operational demand forecasting, long-term climate impacts or production deployment.

Corrected historical labels and archive timestamps do not reconstruct every historical training or settlement vintage. Selective forecast exclusions may affect comparisons. Trading assumes clearing and excludes costs. Researcher exposure to 2025 prevents a fresh-holdout claim for later stages.
